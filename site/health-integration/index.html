HEAL: iOS and Android Health data integration

A concise summary of the integration possibility, cost, data available, supported sources, and the implementation path for bringing health data into HEAL.

1. Summary

  • Possibility: Yes. HEAL can read health and activity data directly through Apple HealthKit on iPhone and Health Connect on Android, using official platform APIs and user-granted permissions.
  • Cost: The platform integrations themselves have no per-user, per-request, or per-record API fee. The cost is the engineering work, store requirements, and any optional third-party service HEAL chooses to add later.
  • Release: Yes, this needs a new version of the app on the App Store and on Google Play. It cannot go out as a silent update: health access is part of the app build itself, and both stores review it. Members get it when they update the app.
  • Data: The first release can read steps, active calories, and workouts. The iOS and Android data fields in view are listed in the Appendix below.
  • Sources: HEAL reads from the health store on the member's phone. Apps and devices such as watches and fitness trackers can feed Apple Health or Health Connect, after which HEAL reads the permitted data from that platform. HEAL does not need a separate integration for every compatible device.

2. Is it possible, and how

Yes. Apple and Google each publish an official, supported way for an app like HEAL to read a member's health data with the member's permission. Nothing in this proposal depends on a workaround or an unofficial tool.

HEAL's app is built with React Native. Open-source libraries already wrap both platforms, so the team does not start from nothing: react-native-health-connect for Android, and @kingstinct/react-native-healthkit or react-native-health for iOS.

2.2 What is not possible

  • Reading without permission. HEAL sees only what the member chooses to share, and only until they withdraw it.
  • The HEAL server collecting the data by itself. There is no server feed. The member's phone has to read and upload.
  • Live updates while the app is closed. Background reading is limited on both platforms, so figures can be hours old.
  • Knowing, on iPhone, that a member refused. A refusal looks the same as having no data.
  • Data from a device that does not write to Apple Health or Health Connect.

2.3 What has to be true for a member

  • They use an iPhone, or an Android phone on Android 9 or later.
  • They agree to share steps, active calories and workouts with HEAL.
  • Their watch or band, if they have one, writes into Apple Health or Health Connect.
  • They open HEAL from time to time, since the app does the syncing.

3. Is it free, and how

Yes, for the platform access itself. Neither Apple nor Google charges for reading health data this way. There is no fee per member, per request or per record. The data is read on the member's own phone, so no usage bill comes from either company.

5. What the platforms require of us

These are fixed facts about the two platforms. They are not choices, and the design has to work within them.

#

Platform fact

What it means for HEAL

1

No server-to-server feed. HealthKit and Health Connect both keep data on the device. Health Connect has no cloud API.

The work splits in two: a sync module inside the app, and an ingestion step in the backend.

2

Background reading is limited. iOS wakes the app at most once per period, and steps are capped at hourly. Android reads only in the foreground unless the member grants a separate Background Read permission.

Data can be hours old. Sync every time the app opens and show when the figures were last updated.

3

Permissions are partial and sometimes invisible. On iOS the app cannot tell a denied read from "no data". Members can limit how much history they share. On Android only the 30 days before permission are readable by default.

We need a "connected, but nothing received yet" state, and a clear way to disconnect.

4

The same activity can arrive more than once. A phone and a watch both count steps. Several apps can write the same workout.

Read steps and calories through the platform's own totals, which remove duplicates, and never by adding raw records.

5

Records get edited and deleted. Android's change tokens expire if unused for 30 days, and a deletion arrives as an ID only.

Store each record's platform ID and source, so a later change updates the same row.

6

Both stores review health access. Apple forbids using health data for advertising and requires disclosure of the specific data collected. Google requires a declaration per data type, an approved use case and an in-app privacy policy.

Start the store paperwork early. "Fitness, wellness and coaching" is the Google use case that fits HEAL.

7

It ships as a new app version. Health access is built into the app itself: the HealthKit capability and its permission wording on iOS, the Health Connect permissions on Android, and the native libraries on both. None of that can be changed by a silent over-the-air update.

A new build goes through App Store review and Google Play review, with Google's Health apps declaration filled in. Members get the feature only after they update the app.

7. Stage 2: what needs a decision

All ten decisions are settled.

7.1 The calorie rule

When a device gives HEAL a calorie figure, HEAL uses it as given and does not recalculate it. That covers a workout's calories and the day's active calories. A watch's "active calories" for the day already includes walking and workouts. So HEAL does not add device workouts or device steps on top of that figure.

Decision (D1):

  • Member has a wearable that sends active calories: use the device's daily figure as Active Burned. Show steps and workouts as detail only.
  • Member has a phone only, sending steps and no calories: keep HEAL's current formula, with device steps replacing the assumed or typed steps.
  • Never use the device's "total calories". It includes resting burn, which HEAL already adds.
  • If the member logs something the device already sent: the agent does not log it a second time without checking. It tells the member that the activity, or the day's steps, has already come in from their device, and asks whether to add it on top, replace what the device sent, or leave it as it is. For example: "Your watch already logged a 32 minute run this morning. Is this the same run, or another one?"

This also settles two open issues: repeated step logs adding up (the device gives one daily total), and the workout intensity bug for any workout a device recorded (the device's own calorie figure replaces the default value).

8. Risks and what is not yet verified

  • Devices outside the two platforms are not covered. A band that does not write into Apple Health or Health Connect will not sync. Support for those can be discussed later, according to need and numbers.
  • The backend's internals are not yet reviewed. This proposal is based on its interface only, and the backend contract has still to be agreed.
  • Background sync is best effort on both platforms. Figures will sometimes be hours behind. D10 is how we are honest about that.
  • Store review can take longer than the build. The data list (D4) is settled, so it can start now.
  • Spotting a repeat log depends on timing. The agent can only ask "is this the same workout?" if the device's record has reached HEAL before the member mentions it. If the sync is behind, the question comes too late.
  • The legal position is from secondary sources. It needs confirming before the consent text is final.
  • Synced data cannot be deleted in the app (D9). The legal review (C4) should confirm that this is acceptable.
